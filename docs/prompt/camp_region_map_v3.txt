A wide regional map of a dry land apocalypse camp, high three-quarter isometric view, one continuous outdoor ground, thick dark-ink outlines, flat storybook colors, two-step cel shadows, small big-head survivors, same drawing method as the reference.

Geography is one believable place, read from far to near.

Far distance, only on the right half: a hazy band of leaning ash-gray apartment ruins under a storm-cloud gray-blue sky. The ruins are small, dusty, and stand behind a wide field of broken concrete.

One broken highway belongs to those ruins. It leaves the apartments as cracked asphalt on the ground, runs leftward across the bare dirt, passes a line of rusted cars the color of old iron, then rises on a short row of concrete pillars with a collapsed ramp that sits on the dirt. The ramp, the pillars, and the asphalt are one connected road. The elevated piece is short and on the right, next to the apartments.

Left side, separated from the apartments by that open dirt and the road: a forest rooted along a creek. Dusty olive trees and dark pine green stand deep inside the woods. The edge facing the road turns into dead gray trunks and sparse scrub, so the living trees thin out before the open ground. A wooden hunting stand and stacked logs sit inside the woods, on the forest side of the dead trunks. Apartment walls stay far from the canopy.

A narrow creek the color of milky tea leaves the forest, crosses the lower ground, and runs to the right edge. Old-bamboo reeds, a plank weir, and a row of metal buckets mark a water spot.

Center, a small warm pad of packed dirt the color of dry wheat straw: a worn school-bus-yellow command bus with a roof dish, a wooden smokehouse with a kerosene-orange stove glow, galvanized water drums, two small survivors in a red tank top and a faded yellow vest, and one empty fresh-lumber foundation. Lantern-orange light covers only this pad.

Lower left near the creek: a tiny cabbage-green garden and tomato-red berry bushes. Lower right: separate rubble piles, a junk heap of plastic crates, and a second empty lumber pad. Dirt paths link the camp to the creek, the forest edge, and the highway ramp.

The forest and the apartment ruins have open ground between them. The highway is a road you could walk from the ruins down the ramp onto the dirt. Pure illustration, one camera, one wide scene.
